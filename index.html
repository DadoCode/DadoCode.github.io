Approach

What interests me is the inside of a system. How information moves through it, where decisions get stuck, what costs more than it should, and what people are still doing by hand because nobody built the alternative.

I am a chemical engineer by training, which is where I learned to break something complicated into measurable parts, model it under constraints and argue about the trade-offs. That turned out to transfer. A building wasting energy, a kiln that will not reach conversion, a company paying for storage nobody measured, a business run across fourteen spreadsheets. Different subjects, same questions.

The useful part is rarely the tool. It is being willing to go and find out how the thing really works before proposing anything, and then saying what the numbers actually show.

Information system · AI-assisted build

Property Operations & Analytics Dashboard

Livein daily use; public code with synthetic data

A short-let operation had outgrown its spreadsheets. Bookings on the platforms, costs in one sheet, documents in a folder, bank records somewhere else. The problem was not a missing dashboard, it was that the operating information of the business was fragmented and no figure could be traced back to anything.

Designed an internal system around the workflow instead of around the spreadsheets. Bookings, expenses, documents, property performance, targets and reporting in one place, with revenue, profit, occupancy, ADR and RevPAR derived from the records rather than cached, and every figure traceable to its source. Documents go upload, extract, review, confirm, and anything confirmed can be undone.

The hard part was the accounting rather than the charts. A property-month can carry revenue from two sources, so each month has one active source and a stay crossing a month boundary splits across both. Managed flats count only the management fee, never the owner's revenue. Get that wrong and every number above it is quietly wrong.

Built with Claude Code. The application is Python, Flask and SQLite; my work was the operational problem, the business rules, how information should flow, the KPI logic, the product and UX direction, and iterating it until it was actually useful. The public repository runs on synthetic data.

Organisational system · operations, London

A company is a system too, and nobody had measured this one

40%cut in contractor spend

30+ tenanted properties, and around them tenants, landlords, contractors, engineers, building management, insurers and solicitors. Costs were being paid because they had always been paid, not because anyone had checked them.

Benchmarked the maintenance suppliers against the incumbent and replaced them, which took roughly 40% off contractor spend. Inventoried what was actually in storage, compared the space against what was being paid for, and restructured it: about 73% off the monthly storage bill.

The rest of it is running the operation end to end rather than task by task. Incidents, insurance claims, renovations, move-ins and move-outs, with the same habit underneath: find out how it actually works before changing it.

Mathematical system · MSc thesis, UCL

Tyre pyrolysis in a rotary kiln, heated from the inside

1270kJ/kg · down from 1400

A wall-heated kiln loses energy through the shell. Would induction-heated stainless steel susceptors sitting inside the bed heat it more efficiently?

Built both as 1-D models in MATLAB and compared them on conversion, temperature profile and specific energy demand rather than on the idea.

The susceptors improved early heat transfer and cut specific energy from 1,400 to 1,270 kJ/kg, but conversion fell from 100% to 88.5%. That is a trade-off, not an improvement, and the thesis says so. Following the result when it contradicts the premise is the part worth keeping.

Physical system · energy consulting, 20+ sites

Where is the energy going, and which fix pays for itself first?

3.3year payback

Hotels, warehouses, a university campus and government buildings across the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Lebanon. Every client knew the bills were high. None of them knew which fix paid for itself first, or whether any of them did at all.

Baselines built from utility bills and logger data, each measure sized against the measured baseline rather than a manufacturer's claim, priced through a bill of quantities, then appraised on NPV, IRR and payback with avoided carbon alongside. Solar PV modelled in HelioScope up to around 410 kWp.

184,000 kWh a year identified at one site and 579,000 at another, ranked so the client knew what to do first. The audit work ran to ISO 50002, EN 16247 and ASHRAE 211, and I wrote the proposals and delivered the training in English and Arabic.

Experimental system · research internship, Barcelona

Why the first seven runs produced nothing

24experiments

Synthesising a biodegradable packaging film from cellulose. Seven attempts in, still no film, and I had spent all of them changing the separation method.

Ran FTIR on the product instead of guessing again. It matched plain cellulose, which meant the reaction had never started and the separation was never the problem.

Catalyst loading in the published method was too low. Raised it, and every run afterwards produced film. Seventeen of the twenty-four worked once the real fault was found.
